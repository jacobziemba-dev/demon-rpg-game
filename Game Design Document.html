Chapter the First

The High Concept

A dark monster-tamer in the lineage of Shin Megami Tensei and Pokémon — where every creature you capture once fed on the living, and your bond with them is written in blood.

The Pitch · 60 Seconds

You are a demon hunter who discovers you can not only slay demons, but bind them — forcing them into service with ritual chains. Travel a blighted world, capture a team of demonic familiars, and battle your way toward an ancient evil that stirs beneath the cathedral cities. A grim, top-down 2D RPG built in Godot 4 for solo release.

The Three Pillars

I · Dread

Every encounter should feel dangerous. Random battles are not filler — they are attrition. Resources are scarce, resurrection is costly, and no demon is truly loyal.

II · Covenant

The bond with each demon is a negotiated contract, not a friendship. They obey while their terms are met. Violate the pact and they turn on you.

III · Study

Knowledge is a weapon. Learning a demon's true name, its weaknesses, and its origins unlocks more power than raw levels ever will.

Synopsis

The Veil tore fifteen years ago. What crossed through was not one kind of demon but many — feral horrors from beneath the earth, fallen seraphim from above, nameless things from the spaces between. The old order fell. The Inquisition was swallowed whole. Now a handful of Binders walk the ruined provinces, carrying chains forged from their own bone-dust, hunting demons not only to kill them but to take them as servants.

You are the youngest Binder in a generation. Your mentor is dead. The pact-chain around your neck is heavier than it has any right to be. And somewhere beneath the drowned cathedral of Saint Molech, something is calling every demon in the world home.

Unique Selling Points

  1. The Pact System. Every caught demon has a contract clause. Break it and they leave — or worse.
  2. True Name Mechanic. Research in-world tomes to learn a demon's true name and permanently weaken or empower it.
  3. Attrition, not Encounter Design. HP/MP carry between fights. No free heals. The dungeon is the battle.
  4. Fusion Rituals. Combine two demons at a summoning circle to birth a third — inheriting skills, losing levels.
  5. One sinner, one solo dev. Scope is ruthlessly controlled: 60 demons, 6 regions, ~15 hours.

Target Audience

Primary

  • SMT / Persona fans who miss dungeon-crawling
  • Players of Siralim, Monster Sanctuary, Cassette Beasts
  • Dark-fantasy RPG readers (Berserk, Dark Souls lore-heads)

Secondary

  • Pokémon veterans looking for something tonally braver
  • Retro-JRPG enthusiasts (EarthBound, Dragon Quest III)
  • Lore-forward streamers and ARG communities

Exploration & The Light

The world is top-down 2D, tile-based, navigated with 8-direction movement. The player carries Lantern Oil; each step in dungeons consumes a fraction of a unit. When the lantern dies, encounter rate triples and certain demons gain ambush priority.

This is the primary attrition pressure — not HP, not MP, but light.

Design Intent · The Lantern

Lantern Oil converts abstract dungeon tension into a concrete, legible resource the player can plan around. It also gives the Chapel a meaningful pull: running out of oil means retreat. We never want the player to feel they can stand still forever and grind safely. The dark moves. The dark is hungry.

The Pact System

Every demon you bind signs a contract. The contract has one clause — a condition you must uphold — and one price you pay while they serve.

Sample Clauses

  • Hunger: must kill one enemy per dungeon, or the demon leaves
  • Vanity: must be in front row during boss fights
  • Wrath: refuses to target a specific demon type
  • Piety: cannot enter holy ground
  • Secrecy: only fights when party HP is below 50%

Sample Prices

  • Tithe: –10% max HP while demon is in roster
  • Murmur: all shops cost +15%
  • Blight: party regen disabled
  • Shadow: lantern oil consumed 25% faster
  • Debt: owe demon a true name upon release

Pacts are renegotiable at the Chapel, at a cost in Lore. Breaking a pact turns the demon hostile — permanently, with a level penalty to all future binds of its kind.

The Binding Ritual (Capture)

Binding Minigame · in-battle
 1. Weaken demon below 25% HP
 2. Use "Bind" action — triggers ritual overlay
 3. Three rotating sigils appear; stop each on its weak glyph
    ├─ hit all three  → automatic bind
    ├─ hit two        → stat-check vs. demon's WILL
    └─ hit one/zero   → demon breaks free, +1 rage stack
 4. If bound, select pact clause + price from 2 random offers
 5. Demon enters roster at level = (its lvl − 2), keeps skills

Chapel · The Home Base

The Chapel is a derelict shrine the player restores over the course of the game. It unlocks slots gradually and is the only place to:

  • Heal fully without consuming a Candle
  • Fuse demons at the summoning circle
  • Research true names in the library
  • Swap roster (hold 6, stable holds 24)
  • Upgrade lantern (capacity, brightness, oil types)
  • Renegotiate pacts at the confessional

Chapter the Third

The Rite of Combat

Turn-based. Menu-driven. Weakness is the only currency that matters.

Overview

Combat is a turn-based, party-vs-party system inspired by the Press Turn rules of Shin Megami Tensei III. Four allies, up to four enemies, fought on a dark vignette screen with the party silhouetted against a sigil circle.

The Press-Turn Economy

Each side begins its round with N press icons, where N = number of conscious combatants on that side. Actions consume icons at different rates:

Action OutcomeIcons Consumed
Normal hit1 full icon
Critical hit / hit a weakness½ icon (gain momentum)
Miss2 icons (squandered)
Nullified (resist/immune)2 icons
Absorbed / reflectedAll remaining icons lost
Use an item1 full icon
Guard / Observe½ icon

The round ends only when one side has spent all icons. One competent weakness-press can double a side's turn count. One botched cast can end it.

Affinities — The Seven Humours

  • Fire — burns, removes ice armor
  • Ice — chills, slows turn order
  • Shadow — drains MP, blinds
  • Radiant — purifies, extra vs. Fallen
  • Blood — costs HP to cast, ignores defense
  • Rot — DOT; stacks; weak to Fire
  • Ætheric — raw damage, no resistances exist

Every demon has an affinity profile: weak / neutral / resist / null / absorb / reflect for each humour. Striking a weak spot grants half-icon and status advantage. Striking null wastes 2 icons. Striking reflect ends your round.

Action Menu

Combat UI · action hierarchy
 ┌─ ATTACK ─────── basic strike, weapon-typed
 ├─ SKILL ──────── demon ability (MP cost)
 │                  ├─ Offensive   (affinity-tagged)
 │                  ├─ Support     (buff/debuff)
 │                  └─ Ritual      (scene-wide, slow)
 ├─ BIND ───────── capture attempt (see §II)

 ├─ ITEM ────────── consumables from pouch
 ├─ GUARD ───────── −50% incoming, +MP regen

Statuses

Conditions

  • Burning · HP ×3 turns
  • Chilled · SPD ÷2
  • Blind · accuracy −50%
  • Bleeding · HP on action
  • Rotting · stacking DOT

Mental

  • Charmed · acts for other side
  • Silenced · no Skills
  • Panic · random target
  • Hexed · no Rituals
  • Despair · halves healing

Unique

  • Marked · next hit +50%
  • Bound · cannot flee
  • Blessed · first hit nullified
  • Anointed · status-immune
  • Named · affected by True Name

Example Round — Worked

Scenario · Binder + 3 demons vs. 2 Rotfiends + 1 Cherub

Party icons: 4. Player opens with Observe on the Cherub → reveals weak: Shadow. (½ icon; 3.5 left.)

Imp casts Umbral Lance on Cherub → hits weakness. (½ icon; 3 left.) Cherub gains Marked.

Binder strikes Cherub with rite-blade → crit via Marked. (½ icon; 2.5 left.) Cherub falls.

Rotfiend #1 resists Shadow; Hound uses Rend (physical) → normal hit. (1 icon; 1.5 left.)

Last demon casts Pyre on both Rotfiends → hits weakness ×2. (½ icon; 1 left.) Both burning.

Binder uses final icon to Guard. Enemy begins with 2 icons. DOT ticks before their round.

Outcome: two enemies down in one round via weakness-press chain. This is the feel we want.

Fusion Rules

  • Fuse two demons at the Chapel summoning circle — requires Salt × 3 + Bone-dust × 2.
  • Result determined by order × order lookup table (64 entries) + level sum.
  • Result level = floor((A.lvl + B.lvl) / 2) − 1.
  • Result inherits one skill per parent, chosen by player.
  • ~5% chance of miscast — both demons lost, player takes Blood damage, no result.
  • Some recipes are locked until specific True Names are researched.

Progression & Economy

Three ladders climb in parallel: the Binder's skill, the demons' power, and the Chapel's grandeur.

The Three Ladders

I · Binder

Levels 1–40. XP from kills & binds. Unlocks new ritual slots, carrying capacity, pact renegotiation discounts. Narrow growth curve — the Binder is never overpowered.

II · Demons

Each demon levels 1–50. XP from combat use. Learns skills at set levels. Fusion resets level but keeps one inherited skill.

III · Chapel

Ranks I–VI. Advanced via Bone-dust deposits + quest completions. Unlocks fusion, stable slots, oil tiers, confession booth.

The True Name Economy

True Names are soft unlocks. Each is a permanent, roster-wide advantage tied to a specific demon. Learning one consumes 50–200 Lore at the library and sometimes requires an in-world key item (a torn page, a priest's confession, etc.).

Effect Template

When you learn [Demon]'s True Name:

  • Bind rate against this demon +30%
  • You deal +15% damage to this demon
  • Unlocks this demon as a fusion result
  • If in your party, the demon's pact price is halved
  • Some bosses gain a new weakness

Difficulty Levers

Encounter rate
Lantern oil remaining × region curse level
Enemy level floor
Scales per region, capped at binder +3
Bind resistance
Demon WILL + (its level − player level) × 2
Death penalty
Lose 10% Lore, respawn at last shrine, demons keep XP
Permadeath mode
Optional "Apostate" difficulty — demons lost forever on faint

Player Expression

There is no class system. The Binder's identity emerges from three choices:

  1. Roster composition. Do you lean on one Order or mix widely? Specialists press harder; mixers survive surprises.
  2. Pact tolerance. How much price are you willing to pay for power? A high-cost roster is lethal but fragile to resource swings.
  3. Research vs. grind. Spend time reading, or spend time killing? Lore wins late; levels win early.

Data Resources

Every demon, skill, item, and pact clause is a Resource subclass with an exported .tres file. Editors author data in Godot's inspector — no external spreadsheets needed, though CSV export is supported for balance pass.

# demon_data.gd — resource schema
class_name DemonData extends Resource

@export var id: StringName
@export var display_name: String
@export var true_name: String
@export var order: Order           # enum: FALLEN, CRAWLER, ...
@export var rarity: Rarity
@export_range(1, 50) var base_level: int = 1

@export var affinities: Dictionary  # humour → weak/resist/null/absorb
@export var skills: Array[SkillData]

Combat Turn Resolver

Combat is a state machine driven by a PressTurnManager node. Actions are Resource-typed and processed through a small rule pipeline. The pipeline is the only place where affinity math lives.

# press_turn_manager.gd (excerpt)
func resolve_action(actor, action, target) -> ActionResult:
    var result := ActionResult.new()
    var affinity := _get_affinity(target, action.humour)

    match affinity:
        Aff.WEAK:    result.icon_cost = 0.5; result.mult = 1.5
        Aff.NEUTRAL: result.icon_cost = 1.0; result.mult = 1.0
        Aff.RESIST:  result.icon_cost = 1.0; result.mult = 0.5
        Aff.NULL:    result.icon_cost = 2.0; result.mult = 0.0
        Aff.ABSORB:  result.icon_cost = 2.0; result.mult = -1.0
        Aff.REFLECT: result.icon_cost = current_icons; result.mult = 0.0
                     result.reflect_to = actor

    if randf() < action.crit_chance:
        result.icon_cost = 0.5
        result.mult *= 2.0

    SignalBus.action_resolved.emit(result)
    return result
